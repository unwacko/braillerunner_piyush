Blind writers who shaped literature

Great blind authors and their works

Blind poets, scholars and memoirists have written some of the world's best-loved books. Many of their works, and countless works by others, are read in braille today. Here are some of the best, starting with India.

Indian authors

From bhakti poetry to the modern memoir

Surdas

c. 1478 – 1583 · Braj Bhasha

The bhakti poet whose songs to Krishna are still sung across North India. Tradition holds he was blind, and his verses about Krishna's childhood are some of the most vivid in Hindi literature.

  • Sur Sagar
  • Sur Saravali
  • Sahitya Lahari

Jagadguru Rambhadracharya

b. 1950 · Sanskrit, Hindi

Lost his sight at two months old and learned scripture by listening. He has written more than a hundred works and founded a university for students with disabilities in Chitrakoot.

  • ShribhargavaraghaviyamSanskrit epic, Sahitya Akademi Award 2005
  • ArundhatiHindi epic, 1994
  • AshtavakraHindi epic, 2010

Ved Mehta

1934 – 2021 · English

Lost his sight to meningitis at three, studied at the Dadar School for the Blind in Bombay, and became a staff writer at The New Yorker for over 30 years. His memoir series Continents of Exile tells the story of his family and of India.

  • Face to Face1957
  • Daddyji1972
  • Vedi1982
  • Sound-Shadows of the New World1986

Ravindra Jain

1944 – 2015 · Hindi

Born blind in Aligarh, he became one of Hindi cinema's best-loved lyricists and composers, writing both the words and the music. He received the Padma Shri in 2015.

  • Chitchor1976
  • Ankhiyon Ke Jharokhon Se1978
  • Ram Teri Ganga Maili1985
  • RamayanTV series, 1987

Puttaraj Gawai

1914 – 2010 · Kannada, Sanskrit, Hindi

Blind from infancy, he was a Hindustani musician and scholar who ran an ashram in Gadag that taught music to thousands of blind and poor children. He received the Padma Bhushan in 2008.

  • Over 80 books in Kannada, Sanskrit and Hindi
  • Puranas (lives of saints) in Kannada verse

Irattaiyar

14th century · Tamil

The "twin poets" of Tamil Nadu. Tradition holds that one cousin was blind and the other could not walk, so one carried the other and they composed together.

  • Thillai Kalambakam
  • Ekambaranathar Ula

Authors from around the world

From ancient Greece to the present

Louis Braille

1809 – 1852 · France

Blinded in an accident at three, he invented the six-dot code at fifteen. Every braille system since, Bharati braille included, is built on his cell.

  • Procedure for Writing Words, Music and Plain Song Using Dots1829

Helen Keller

1880 – 1968 · United States

Deafblind from nineteen months, she read in braille, graduated from Radcliffe College and wrote twelve books. She also visited India in 1955.

  • The Story of My Life1903
  • Optimism1903
  • The World I Live In1908
  • Midstream: My Later Life1929

John Milton

1608 – 1674 · England

Completely blind by 1652, he dictated his greatest work line by line to helpers.

  • Paradise Lost1667
  • Paradise Regained1671
  • Samson Agonistes1671

Homer

c. 8th century BCE · Greece

Tradition remembers him as a blind bard. His epics were passed on aloud for generations before they were written down.

  • The Iliad
  • The Odyssey

Jorge Luis Borges

1899 – 1986 · Argentina

He lost his sight in the 1950s, around the time he became director of Argentina's National Library, and kept writing by dictation.

  • Ficciones1944
  • The Aleph1949
  • In Praise of Darkness1969
  • The Book of Sand1975

Taha Hussein

1889 – 1973 · Egypt

Blind from early childhood, he became known as the "Dean of Arabic Literature" and later Egypt's minister of education.

  • On Pre-Islamic Poetry1926
  • The Daysautobiography, 1929
  • The Call of the Curlew1934

Abu al-Ala al-Ma'arri

973 – 1057 · Syria

A blind poet and philosopher famous for his bold, questioning verse.

  • Luzumiyyat
  • The Epistle of Forgiveness

Jacques Lusseyran

1924 – 1971 · France

Blinded at seven, he led a student resistance group in occupied Paris as a teenager and survived Buchenwald.

  • And There Was Light1953
